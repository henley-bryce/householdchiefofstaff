# Triage rules

Hard-won rules for deciding what goes where and what to ignore. Apply them silently; mention a rule to the user only when it changes what they see.

## What counts as a decision

A **decision** has an external deadline or a real fork (yes/no, pick a slot, pay by a date, choose between options). It gets one line in Decisions Needed.

Not decisions:
- Pure FYI mail (newsletters, "here's what we did today"). Summarize at most.
- Notes under a decision. Sub-bullets people type under a checklist item are **their notes**, not new decisions. Fold the substance into the Action Items table or the item's one-liner.
- Tasks with an owner and no open question. Those belong in Action Items & Owners.

## Weekly cleanup

- One line per decision. Owners, timing, and status go in the Action Items table.
- Resolved items are cleared. Anything resolved that creates follow-up work leaves behind an Action Item.
- A durable outcome of a decision (for example, a standing plan) moves to Standing Info.

## Checked items with no answer

If a decision is checked off in a meeting but no outcome is written down, do **not** infer one. Leave it open, say "checked off with no recorded outcome," and surface it in the end-of-session summary.

## Owners

Every action item gets an owner. If none was stated, mark it **Unassigned** and flag it as a gap, with suggested timing based on the deadline. Do not silently assign the primary user.

## Recurring check-ins

Some questions repeat weekly and have no standing answer (for example, who handles a recurring early pickup). Keep them as an evergreen checkbox that resets each week. Do not invent a rotation unless the household asks for one.

## Calendar filtering

- A shared or imported partner calendar often mixes work and family. Filter work events by signals (a video-meeting link, corporate project names), then show the user what was filtered and ask for corrections. Do not ask the partner to retag anything.
- Standing family holds that are "if we get to it" (a weekly pool visit, say) are known events. Do not call them out weekly unless they are actually happening.
- Monthly standing events (a neighborhood meetup on the first Wednesday, for example) are easy to filter out by accident. If the user says one was missed, add it to Standing Info so every future pull catches it.

## Email filtering

- Automated daily reports (check-in/check-out confirmations and the like) are noise. Label them, mark them read, and move them out of the inbox. Surface one only if its content contains an actual incident, update, or action item, which means reading it, not just checking the subject.
- Billing that is on autopay is not a decision. Re-flag it only if a charge looks wrong.
- Programs the family has said it is ineligible for stay out of Decisions Needed, even if reminder emails keep arriving. Record the rule in System Notes.

## People and names

- Two people with the same first name: record both in Standing Info with the relationship, and say which one "planning" refers to. An employer's colleague with the same name as a relative is a common collision.
- A person who goes by a nickname: link the nickname and the legal name to one entry.

## Dates

- Build "This Week" from event objects, never a hand-typed table.
- Recurring events (early release, activities) get their weekday from the calendar, not from memory. A wrong recurring weekday propagates everywhere.
- Closure calendars from a district are often "tentative." Say so, and re-check periodically.

## Birthdays and closures

- Birthdays inside about 45 days: flag, and flag when a gift needs to be bought.
- School closures inside 3 to 6 months: flag so childcare can be arranged before the week of.
